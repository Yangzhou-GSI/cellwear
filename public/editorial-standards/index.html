How Cellwear builds trust

Editorial standards.
Every image needs context.

Cellwear turns microscopic imagery into cancer education and design. These standards explain where the information comes from, how imagery is labeled, and what the site does not claim.

ON THIS PAGEScope and purposeSource hierarchyResearch processImage standardsCorrectionsMedical limits

Trust is not created by sounding certain. It comes from showing the source, naming the limits, and correcting the record when stronger evidence appears.

01 / SCOPE

Education first; individual care stays with professionals.

Cellwear explains general concepts in cancer biology, microscopy, pathology, biomarkers, screening, and treatment. The aim is to help readers ask better questions and understand scientific imagery—not to interpret a person’s symptoms, test results, diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment options.

02 / SOURCE HIERARCHY

Primary and authoritative sources come first.

Core medical claims are checked against current pages from public health and research institutions, especially the National Cancer Institute and other NIH institutes. Where appropriate, Cellwear may also use CDC guidance, peer-reviewed research, professional medical organizations, or original institutional image records.

Direct links appear at the end of educational guides so readers can verify a statement and continue learning. Commercial blogs, unsourced social posts, and image captions detached from their original records are not treated as primary evidence.

03 / PROCESS

Each guide follows a visible research path.

01 / DEFINE

Match the question

The page addresses a specific reader question rather than repeating a keyword.

02 / VERIFY

Check the evidence

Medical statements are compared with authoritative sources and linked.

03 / LABEL

Name the image

Specimen, method, subject, creator, source, and rights are recorded when known.

04 / REVIEW

Revisit the page

Pages display a review date and are updated when evidence or guidance changes.

AUTHORSHIP DISCLOSURE

Cellwear Education content is researched and source-linked, but it is not independently reviewed by a physician unless a page explicitly names the reviewer and credentials.

04 / IMAGE INTEGRITY

Scientific images and artistic concepts remain separate.

Microscopy images include a visible credit or a link to the image credits and rights record. Captions identify the specimen or research model and imaging method when that information is available. False color, fluorescent labeling, and artistic treatment are described so viewers do not mistake technique for natural appearance.

Product concepts inspired by cells are labeled as art. They are not presented as diagnostic images. Submitted work must belong to the contributor or include clear permission for use; patient-identifying information is not accepted.

05 / CORRECTIONS

Errors should leave a visible trail.

If a factual, sourcing, attribution, or licensing error is found, Cellwear will correct the page and update its review date. Material corrections should be described on the affected page. Readers and image creators can use the submission contact route to flag an issue or provide stronger source information.

06 / MEDICAL LIMITS

This archive cannot diagnose or direct care.

The site provides general education only. It cannot assess risk, recommend screening for an individual, interpret symptoms, read a pathology report, or select treatment. Urgent or personal health questions belong with a qualified healthcare professional.
