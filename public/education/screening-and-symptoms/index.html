Awareness in action

Screening looks early.
Symptoms ask attention.

Useful awareness avoids two extremes: ignoring a lasting change and assuming every change is cancer.

01 / DIFFERENT STARTING POINTS

Screening begins before symptoms.

SCREENING

Looking for certain cancers early

Screening tests are used in people without symptoms. Which tests are recommended depends on age, risk, history, and current guidance.

SYMPTOMS

Investigating a change

Symptoms can have many causes. A lasting or concerning change deserves evaluation rather than self-diagnosis.

02 / WHAT SCREENING CAN DO

Screening can help detect some cancers earlier.

Established screening tests exist for certain cancers, not every cancer. A screening result is not always a diagnosis. An abnormal result may lead to additional tests, while a normal result cannot guarantee that cancer is absent.

Benefits and possible harms differ by test. Timing depends on factors such as age, personal history, family history, and other risks. A health professional can help apply current guidance to an individual.

03 / HOW TO THINK ABOUT A CHANGE

Symptoms are signals, not conclusions.

Cancer can cause many different symptoms, but those symptoms are often caused by other health conditions. What matters is noticing changes that persist, worsen, feel unusual, or concern you and discussing them with a qualified health professional.

DO NOT WAIT FOR A PERFECT CHECKLIST

Online information cannot tell you what a symptom means. If something has not improved after a few weeks—or feels urgent—tell a trusted adult and seek professional medical care.

04 / THE NEXT STEP

Move from uncertainty to qualified help.

  • Write down what changed and when you first noticed it.
  • Tell a parent, guardian, or another trusted adult if you need help arranging care.
  • Bring questions to a qualified health professional rather than relying on an image, social post, or symptom checker.
  • If a screening test is being considered, ask what it is designed to find and what follow-up could involve.
